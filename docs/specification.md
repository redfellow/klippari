# Klippari specification

This document describes what Klippari does. It is the source of truth:
when the code and this document disagree, one of them has a bug.

## Purpose

Klippari is a background app for macOS that remembers the last things
copied and pastes any of them from the keyboard. Ordinary copy and paste
are unaffected, and every step of invoking, selecting and pasting works
without the mouse.

## Platform

- macOS 15 or later. Swift 6 language mode. No third-party dependencies.
- Bundle identifier `com.arime.Klippari`, version 1.0.
- A background app: no Dock icon, a menu bar icon only.
- Not sandboxed, because pasting into other apps requires synthesizing
  keystrokes.
- Signed with a stable identity and always run from
  `/Applications/Klippari.app`. The Accessibility permission and the
  login item are tied to that signed bundle.

## Capturing

- The general pasteboard's change count is polled every 0.5 seconds.
- Whatever is on the clipboard when the app starts is not recorded.
- On a change, every pasteboard item is read with every type and its
  data. A copy of several files is one history item.
- A copy is not recorded when any type is one of the nspasteboard.org
  markers `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType`,
  `org.nspasteboard.TransientType` or
  `org.nspasteboard.AutoGeneratedType`, or one of the older markers
  `com.agilebits.onepassword`, `de.petermaurer.TransientPasteboardType`,
  `com.typeit4me.clipping` or `Pasteboard generator type`. No data is
  read from such a copy.
- A copy over 20 MB in total is not recorded. Reading stops once the
  running total passes the cap.
- A type whose data cannot be read is dropped. The copy is dropped only
  when nothing is left.
- Each item records its capture time and the name of the app that was
  frontmost when it was captured.
- Copying content identical to an existing item (same types and bytes,
  compared by SHA-256) moves that item to the top instead of adding a
  duplicate. Its original capture time and source app are kept.
- The history keeps at most N items, 50 by default, and drops the
  oldest. Lowering N drops the excess at once.
- What the app itself puts on the clipboard when pasting is never
  recorded as a new copy.
- Capture can be paused. Nothing copied while paused is recorded, also
  not after resuming. Pause is not remembered across restarts.

## Secrets

- A copy is a secret when it comes from a password app (Keychain
  Access, Passwords, Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePassXC, by bundle
  identifier), or when its text contains a credential in a well-known
  format: GitHub tokens (`ghp_` and the other `gh*_`, `github_pat_`),
  OpenAI and Anthropic keys (`sk-`, `sk-ant-`), AWS access key IDs
  (`AKIA`, `ASIA`), Slack tokens (`xox*-`), JWTs, and private keys
  (`-----BEGIN … PRIVATE KEY-----`, PGP's `… PRIVATE KEY BLOCK-----`). There is no entropy guess. The
  source app is the copy's `org.nspasteboard.source` when it has one,
  otherwise the app that was frontmost when the copy was made: when
  another app is activated, the clipboard is checked once more before
  the switch counts, so a copy made just before Cmd+Tab is credited to
  the app it was made in. A copy seen while a password app has been
  frontmost at any moment since the last regular check (every 0.5
  seconds) is a secret whatever its source; a wrongly masked row costs
  less than a password shown in clear.
- A secret is never written to disk. It is kept in memory with its full
  content and leaves the history 10 minutes after it was copied, or
  when the app quits. A timer set for the next secret to go removes it
  on time; waking from sleep, opening the panel and pasting check
  first, so an expired secret is never shown or pasted. Pasting it puts it on the clipboard marked as
  concealed (`org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType`), so that other clipboard
  managers leave it out; it does not extend its time. Copying it again
  does. A copy with the same text as a secret in the history is that
  secret again, from whatever app. Adding a secret takes every item
  with its text out of the history and off the disk, even one copied
  with other types beside the text (rich text from Notes, say), since
  the same password from two apps rarely comes as the same bytes.
  Secrets do not count toward the history size, so copying one never
  pushes a saved item out, and Restore's room leaves them out too.
- Clearing the history drops secrets; they are never archived. A copy
  of a secret's text already in the cleared history, from when it was
  copied as an ordinary item, is deleted when the secret is added, and
  each clear keeps its date. Restoring never brings back an item whose
  text is a secret in the history: its archived copy is deleted
  instead.
- Its row is masked. The first credential on the line keeps its prefix
  and, when long enough, its last four characters (`sk-ant-••••••Q4fZ`),
  so two of the same kind can be told apart. Before it only a variable
  name stays (`export GH_TOKEN=`); any other text before or after it is
  replaced by dots, since a secret often sits beside another one. A line
  with no known credential, such as a password, shows only the dots:
  none of its characters, and not its length. A line that is
  exactly a private key's BEGIN or END header stays. A lock badge shows
  the minutes left.
- The filter matches only what a secret's row shows, and the word
  'secret'. The image preview never shows a secret.
- Cmd+S on a selected secret keeps it as an ordinary item: it is saved
  and shown in full, and copies of the same text are not taken for
  secrets again until the app quits. While a secret is selected, the
  footer's 'Quick paste' hint gives way to 'Not a secret'.
- An OpenAI-style `sk-` key must be at least 32 characters after its
  prefix and mix capitals, small letters and digits, so that URL slugs
  and CSS class names starting with `sk-` are not taken for keys.

## Storage

- History lives in `~/Library/Application Support/Klippari/items.noindex`
  and survives restart and logout. Folders are mode 0700 and files
  0600.
- Every file is encrypted with AES-GCM, with a 256-bit key kept in the
  login keychain (service `com.arime.Klippari`), and bound to its item
  and file name. A new key is made only while neither `items.noindex`
  nor the archive holds an encrypted item. If the key is missing while
  one does, or the keychain refuses, the history is kept for that run
  only, in a folder in Caches with a key that never leaves memory
  (each launch deletes such folders whose run has ended), and
  the saved history is left alone. While that lasts the app says so:
  the menu bar menu starts with the disabled lines 'History isn't being
  saved' and the reason, and the panel's footer shows the same in
  place of the key hints. Try Again in the menu opens the saved
  history as a launch would and moves the run's history into it; if
  it still cannot be opened, the reason is updated.
- When the reason is a missing key, the menu also offers Reset Saved
  History…. After a confirmation that says the old history cannot be
  read and will be deleted, it deletes every sealed item in
  `items.noindex` and the archive, makes a new key and goes on saving,
  without a restart; what is in the history then is saved under the
  new key, secrets excepted, while what was cleared during the run is
  not kept. Items still in plain form need no key: they are kept and
  encrypted with the new one.
  If the key has come back by then, nothing is deleted and the saved
  history opens with it.
- A `key-check` file in the Klippari folder, sealed with the key, is
  written once the history opens with the key and nothing plain is
  left. At launch, a key that does not open it, or (before it exists)
  a key that opens none of the sealed items, is the wrong key: the
  history is not saved that run, for the same reasons, and the menu
  offers Reset Saved History…, which deletes what the key cannot open
  and goes on saving with it. A new key removes an old `key-check`.
- While the history is saved, items on disk that the key cannot open
  are counted: sealed with another key, in no known form, and, once
  `key-check` exists, still plain (version 1.0 run again, or a file
  put there by something else; these are not taken into the
  history). The menu then shows 'N saved items can't be read' and
  Delete Unreadable Items…, which deletes them after a confirmation.
- Spotlight and backups are kept out: the folder names end in
  `.noindex`, which Spotlight skips, and the Klippari folder is
  excluded from backups. It also holds a `.metadata_never_index` file,
  a further hint that Spotlight may not honour outside a volume's
  root.
- Version 1.0 kept the history as plain files in `items`, and builds
  before encryption kept the cleared history in `items-archive`. On
  launch these are renamed to their `.noindex` names and every plain
  item is encrypted in place, in each clear's folder too. This runs off
  the main thread before the history opens: meanwhile the menu says
  'Encrypting the saved history…', the shortcut is not yet registered
  and copies are not recorded (after Try Again or Reset, the run's
  history keeps recording until it is taken over). A plain item
  whose data cannot be read whole is removed; a manifest that is
  neither sealed nor plain JSON is left alone; an item that cannot be
  saved (a full disk) is left as it is and tried again on the next
  launch. A clear
  keeps its date, which is in its folder's name; a flat archive gets
  its 24 hours from the move.
- Each item has its own directory named by its identifier, containing:
  - `manifest.json`: identifier, capture time, last-used time, source
    app, image size and format if any, content hash, and for each
    pasteboard item the list of types with their data file names.
  - `e<item>-t<type>.bin`: the raw bytes of one type. File names come
    from indices, never from the type string.
  - `thumb.png`: for images only, at most 56 pixels on the longer side.
- An item is written to a temporary directory and renamed into place.
  On start, directories that are incomplete (no manifest, or a
  manifest that opens but names data that is missing or damaged) are
  removed and the rest of the history loads. A manifest that is there
  but cannot be read (permissions, a disk error) is kept, and counts as
  sealed when deciding whether a new key may be made. An item in the history
  whose manifest does not decrypt with the key is never deleted: it is
  skipped, and clearing the history leaves it where it is. In the
  cleared history such an item is not counted or restored, and goes
  when its clear expires.
- In memory, an item keeps only its plain text and file URLs. All other
  data is read from disk when the item is pasted.
- Moving an item to the top rewrites only its manifest.
- Clearing the history moves every item's directory into a folder of
  its own for that clear, `items-archive.noindex/<time>/`, beside `items.noindex`. The
  folder is named by the time of the clear in UTC, ISO 8601 basic
  format (`20261120T060413Z`); two clears in the same second share
  one. Earlier clears are kept, so clearing twice loses nothing. An
  archived item with the same content as one cleared now gives way to
  the newer one. An item kept only in memory, because saving it
  failed, is saved into the clear's folder. An item that can be
  neither moved nor saved stays in the history and is left out of the
  count the notice gives, so everything it counts can be restored.
  An archive left flat by earlier builds of this change, with item
  folders directly in `items-archive.noindex`, becomes one clear at launch,
  dated by the folder's modification time. Restoring moves back as many archived
  items as the history has room for (the history size minus the items
  in it), newest last-used time first, and orders everything by
  last-used time. The rest stay in the archive, so a restore never
  deletes an item or pushes one out of the history; an archived copy
  of something already in the history is dropped. An item that could
  not be moved stays in the archive too. A clear's folder is removed
  once it is empty, and `items-archive.noindex` once no clear is left.
  Secrets are never archived: clearing drops them, and they are not
  counted.
- Each clear is deleted 24 hours after it was made. Klippari checks at
  launch, when the Mac wakes from sleep, and when a single timer set
  for the oldest clear's time fires (it may fire up to a minute late,
  so macOS can group wake-ups). The timer is set again after every
  clear, restore, delete and check, never for less than a minute
  ahead, so a clear that cannot be deleted is tried again each minute
  rather than at once; with nothing cleared there is no timer. Delete in Settings deletes every clear at once.
- The archive may take at most 1 GB. When a clear takes it past that,
  the oldest clears are deleted until it fits, but never the clear
  just made, so it can always be restored.
- Items are ordered by last-used time, newest first.

## The panel

- Opened with the global shortcut. It takes keyboard focus without
  activating Klippari, so the app the user was in stays frontmost and
  receives the paste.
- It appears on the screen under the mouse pointer. Until it has been
  moved it is horizontally centred with its top edge a quarter of the
  way down the screen.
- It can be dragged by any part of the card that is not a control. The
  spot it is left in is remembered across restarts as a position
  relative to the screen, so it reopens in the same relative place on
  whichever screen is in use. The card is always kept fully on screen.
- With the **Position** setting on, it opens next to the text field
  being typed in instead, read through Accessibility when the shortcut
  is pressed: centred on the field, 12 points above it when the card
  fits there, otherwise 12 points below it, so it never covers the
  field, and always fully on screen. A field over 200 points tall, such
  as an editor, counts as just the caret's line. When no text field can
  be read (no Accessibility permission, nothing focused, or an app that
  hides its fields), it goes next to the mouse pointer the same way.
  The whole lookup takes at most 0.2 seconds; past that, or when a call
  times out, the panel goes next to the pointer, so a busy app does not
  hold it up.
  A focused element counts as a text field by its role, or by a caret
  with a height: with VoiceOver running, Electron apps give buttons an
  empty caret.
  Electron apps expose their fields only while an assistive app has
  turned their accessibility on (VoiceOver sets
  `AXEnhancedUserInterface`). Klippari does not ask for it
  (`AXManualAccessibility`): the fields appear about two seconds later,
  far past the 0.2 seconds, the request reads back as off even while it
  is on, and while it is on the app keeps its whole accessibility tree
  up to date, which costs it time and memory and can change how it
  behaves. So in an Electron app the panel goes by the text field when
  VoiceOver or another assistive app is running, and by the pointer
  otherwise.
  Opened above the pointer, the card grows upward if the list grows
  while it is open. Where it is dragged to is then not remembered.
- Each time it opens the list is scrolled so the selected row is in
  view.
- It closes when it loses focus.
- It follows the system light or dark appearance, using a native blur
  material and the system font.

Layout, top to bottom, in a card 640 points wide with 14-point corners:

- Filter field with a magnifier glyph, the placeholder 'Type to
  filter', and a count on the right: '50 items', '1 item', or '2 of 50'
  while filtering.
- The list: rows 44 points high, at most 9 visible (every row
  that Cmd+1 to Cmd+9 can paste), scrolling to keep
  the selection in view.
- A strip of key hints, each kept on one line: Return 'Paste',
  Shift+Return 'Plain text', Cmd+1–9 'Quick paste' on the left; Cmd+Backspace 'Delete',
  followed by Shift 'all', and esc 'Close' on the right. A notice replaces the hints when there is
  something to say, and so does the warning that the history is not
  being saved, for as long as it is not.

Each row has four lanes:

1. Number, 1 to 9 for the first nine visible rows, blank after that.
2. An icon for the kind of item, or a thumbnail for an image.
3. The title on one line, followed by a grey tag. A title too long for
   the row loses its middle, so its start and end stay in view. A
   secret has a lock badge with its minutes left after it.
4. Source app name, left-aligned, and age, right-aligned. The app
   column is as wide as the longest name shown, up to 110 points.

The selected row is the only coloured element: blue `#0A5CFF` with
white text, the same blue as the app icon.

Row content, first match wins:

| Item | Title | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One image file with its own PNG data | 'Image' | '1280 × 720 · PNG' |
| One or more files | File name, or 'N files' | 'file' |
| Image data (PNG or TIFF) | 'Image' | '1280 × 720 · PNG' |
| Text that also has RTF or HTML | First non-empty line | 'styled' |
| Text that is a single http(s) URL | The URL | none |
| Other text | First non-empty line | 'N lines' when more than one non-empty line |
| Anything else | The type's readable name | none |

The first row covers screenshot helpers such as clipaste, which offer
an image to terminals as its PNG bytes plus a file URL and path for the
same image saved to disk. A file copied in Finder carries its icon as
TIFF, not PNG, so it stays a file. The file name of such an image still
matches the filter.

Ages read 'now' under a minute, then 'N min', 'N h', 'N d'.

With no history the list shows 'Nothing copied yet'. With a filter
that matches nothing it shows 'No matches'.

## Keyboard

| Key | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Shift+Cmd+V (configurable) | Open the panel with the newest item selected and the filter focused. Pressed again while open, move the selection down. |
| Up / Down, Ctrl+P / Ctrl+N | Move the selection. It stops at either end. |
| Return | Paste the selected item as it was copied. |
| Shift+Return | Paste it as plain text. An item with no text form is pasted as copied. |
| Cmd+1 to Cmd+9 | Paste that row of the visible list, as copied. |
| Any typing | Edit the filter. |
| Cmd+Backspace | Remove the selected item from the history. The selection moves to the next row, or the previous one at the end. |
| Shift+Cmd+Backspace | Clear the whole history into the archive, without asking, since it can be restored. The notice says 'Cleared N items. Restore them in Settings.', and how many secrets were removed when there were any, since they cannot be restored. The menu's Clear History asks first and counts the two apart in the same way. |
| Esc | Clear a non-empty filter; otherwise close the panel without pasting. |

The filter matches case-insensitively against an item's text, its file
names and its source app name. An image, which has no text, also
matches 'image' and its format, such as 'png'. The first match becomes
selected and rows are renumbered.

If something is copied while the panel is open, the list updates and
the selection stays on the same item. If the selected item leaves the
list by itself, as a secret does when its time is up, the row that
takes its place is selected, or the one before it at the end.

With the **Mouse** setting on, moving the pointer over a row selects it,
and a click pastes it as copied; Shift+click pastes it as plain text.
Only a pointer that moves selects: a row that scrolls under a still
pointer while moving with the keyboard, or the row under the pointer
when the panel opens, does not take the selection. Selecting with the
pointer never scrolls the list. With the setting off, rows ignore the
mouse.

With the **Images** setting on, whenever the selected row is an image,
the image is shown large beside the card, so similar screenshots can
be told apart while moving through the list with the keyboard. While
the pointer is over another image row's thumbnail, that image is shown
instead.

- The preview never covers the card. It goes to the right of the card,
  or to the left when that side has room for a larger preview, centred
  on the highlighted row and following it as the selection moves or the
  list scrolls, kept on screen. Only when neither side has room for
  a useful preview does it go below or above the card.
- It is at most 320 × 240 points, never enlarged past the image's own
  size, and kept 8 points inside the screen's edges.
- It appears at once. Previews of the image rows are made in the
  background when the list changes and kept, up to 20; one not made yet
  shows the stored thumbnail until it is ready.
- It moves with the panel when the panel is dragged, takes no clicks,
  and closes with the panel.

## Pasting

- The item's full content is read from disk and written to the general
  pasteboard, the panel closes, and 50 milliseconds later Cmd+V is
  posted to the system.
- A plain-text paste writes only the item's plain string. The history
  item keeps its styled form.
- When an item has image data and no text, its HTML type is left out
  of the paste. Browsers attach an HTML fragment that only points at
  the remote original, and rich text views that prefer it can block
  while fetching it. The stored item keeps the HTML.
- The pasted item becomes the current clipboard content and moves to
  the top of the history.
- Posting Cmd+V requires the Accessibility permission. Without it the
  item is still put on the clipboard, the panel stays open with the
  notice 'Copied. Klippari needs Accessibility to paste directly.'
  followed by the link 'Open Accessibility Settings', and the system
  permission prompt is shown once per install. Clicking anywhere on the
  notice opens System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.

## Menu bar

- The icon is the app icon's three rows as a monochrome template
  image, so macOS tints it for light and dark menu bars. It is dimmed
  while capture is paused.
- Menu: Pause Capture / Resume Capture, Clear History, Reset Panel
  Position, Settings… (Cmd+,), Quit Klippari (Cmd+Q). While the
  history is not being saved, two disabled lines come first: 'History
  isn't being saved' and why (the key is missing, the keychain did not
  give it, or the folder could not be set up), followed by Reset Saved
  History… when the key is missing.
- Reset Panel Position forgets where the panel was dragged to, so it
  opens in its default place again.
- Clear History asks for confirmation, then clears the history into
  the archive.

## Settings

- **History size**: 5 to 500 items, 50 by default.
- **Shortcut**: recording starts on Return, Space or a click in the
  field, and Esc cancels. A shortcut needs Cmd, Ctrl or Option. Cmd
  alone with A, Z, X, C, V, Q or W, and Shift+Cmd+Z, are refused,
  because a global shortcut swallows its keystroke in every app. If a
  shortcut cannot be registered, the previous one is kept and the
  field says so.
- **Start at login**: on by default. The app registers itself once, on
  first launch, and only when running from `/Applications`. The toggle
  turns it off or on.
- **Mouse**: point at a row to select it, click to paste it. Off by
  default.
- **Position**: open the panel next to the text field being typed in,
  or else the mouse pointer. Off by default.
- **Images**: enlarge the selected image beside the panel. Off by
  default.
- **Cleared history**: the number of items in the cleared history and
  the date of the clear ('cleared …', or 'last cleared …' when it
  comes from more than one), with Restore and Delete below and the
  note 'Each clear is deleted 24 hours after it was made.'. When a restore leaves items
  behind because the history is full, a line under the buttons says
  how many were restored and how many did not fit, or that nothing
  was restored, and suggests raising Keep last. Items that could not
  be moved back are counted on that line too, as still in the cleared
  history. The row is shown only while there is a cleared history.

History size, shortcut, panel position and the panel options are
stored in the app's preferences.

## App icon

Three rounded rows on a near-black rounded square: the top row blue
`#0A5CFF`, the two below in receding greys. It is the panel in
miniature. `Scripts/make-icon.swift` draws it at every size.

## Building and testing

- `Scripts/build-app.sh` builds a release, assembles and signs
  `Klippari.app`, installs it to `/Applications` and launches it.
  `--no-install` stops after building. The signing identity must be
  given, with `--identity <name>` or the `CODESIGN_IDENTITY`
  environment variable; none is stored in the repository, and the
  script stops with an error without one.
- `swift test` runs the unit tests of the core library, which holds
  everything that does not need a window: capture rules, storage,
  history, row presentation, filtering and panel state.
- `docs/manual-checklist.md` lists the checks that need a person at
  the Mac.
- `Klippari --snapshot <png> [dark] [--query <text>]` renders the
  panel with sample items to an image, for checking its layout. Adding
  `settings` renders the settings window's content instead.

## Not included

- Sync between machines.
- Pinned items, snippets, or editing an item's content.
- Excluding particular apps from capture.
- Selecting or pasting rows with the mouse.
- App Store distribution.
